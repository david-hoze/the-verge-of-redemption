# Two Objects of Suspicion

## The Talmud and Chabad-Lubavitch in the Conspiracist Imagination

*Shutter Island (2010). Directed by Martin Scorsese. Starring Leonardo DiCaprio, Mark Ruffalo, Ben Kingsley. A U.S. marshal investigating a vanished patient at a hospital for the criminally insane finds that every locked door and evasive answer deepens the conspiracy he is uncovering - until it turns out he assembled the plot himself, inside a frame where even a denial counts as one more thing the conspiracy would arrange. The method that reads both the evidence and its absence as proof.*

---

_A prefatory note on occasion and method._ This essay began as a response to a question put to me in a comment thread: "What do you think about the Talmud? Chabad Lubavitch?" The question was asked beneath an article arguing that Western civilization has been at war with Judaism for two millennia, and whose recommended bibliography included _The Protocols of the Elders of Zion_, Henry Ford's _The International Jew_, and _Mein Kampf_.

I want to treat the question seriously anyway — not because I expect to persuade the asker, but because the question has a structure worth exposing, and because the two objects named have real histories that are more interesting than either their detractors or their defenders usually allow. What follows is not apologetics. Where the sources are difficult, I say so. An argument that requires the tradition to be blameless is not an argument; it is a hostage situation.

---

## I. The Form of the Question

"What do you think about the Talmud?" is not answerable as posed, and its unanswerability is not incidental — it is the mechanism.

The Talmud is roughly 2,700 folio pages of multi-generational legal and narrative argument, compiled over some three centuries, in two languages, across two imperial jurisdictions, and glossed continuously for the fifteen hundred years since. Asking what one thinks of it is formally similar to asking what one thinks of the common law, or of the Corpus Juris Civilis, or of the collected proceedings of the Roman Senate. There is no proposition to affirm or deny.

But the question is not empty. It is doing work. It requests a verdict on an object whose contents the asker will supply. Whatever answer is given — approval, qualified approval, criticism — the asker retains the right to specify afterward what was being approved or criticized. If I say the Talmud is a great intellectual monument, I have endorsed whatever passage is produced next. If I concede that a particular passage is difficult, the concession is generalized. The question is constructed so that both moves lose.

This is worth naming precisely because the same structure recurs across the literature under discussion. The load-bearing move in nearly every case is a **genre error**: treating a record of argument as a statement of doctrine. Everything else follows from it. Once you have persuaded a reader that the Talmud is a creed, any sentence in it becomes a plank of that creed, and the sheer volume of the corpus guarantees an inexhaustible supply of planks.

So the useful question is not "what do you think of the Talmud" but: _what kind of object is the Talmud, and what would have to be true for a quotation from it to establish what it is claimed to establish?_

---

## II. What Kind of Object the Talmud Is

The Babylonian Talmud consists of the Mishnah — a terse legal code redacted in Roman Palestine around 200 CE — together with the Gemara, a sprawling commentary produced in the rabbinic academies of Sasanian Babylonia and redacted over roughly the third through sixth centuries, with anonymous editorial layers (the _stam_) likely later still.

Three features of its construction matter here.

**First, it is a transcript of dispute.** The characteristic unit is the _sugya_: a passage that raises a question, proposes an answer, objects to the answer, refines it, cites a contradictory tradition, resolves or fails to resolve the contradiction. Minority positions are preserved. Rejected positions are preserved. Positions no authority ever adopted are preserved, sometimes precisely because their refutation is instructive. The Mishnah itself explains that minority views are recorded so that a later court may rely on them (_Eduyot_ 1:5) — an explicit editorial theory of why losing arguments belong in the text.

The consequence is that citing a line from a sugya as "what the Talmud teaches" is not a mild distortion. It is a category mistake of the kind that would be immediately recognized in any other legal corpus. No one reads a dissent in _Plessy_ as American law. No one quotes a rejected objection from a scholastic _quaestio_ as the doctrine of the Church.

**Second, it is not a code, and it was never treated as one.** Practical Jewish law does not run directly from the Talmud. It runs through the Geonim, the Rishonim, and the great codificatory works — Maimonides' _Mishneh Torah_ (c. 1180), the _Arba'ah Turim_, the _Shulchan Arukh_ (1565) with its Ashkenazi glosses, and the subsequent commentarial literature. To learn what Jewish law holds on any question, one consults the codes and the responsa. A Talmudic line that no posek adopted has no more legal standing than an abandoned draft.

**Third, the text has a censorship history that runs in both directions.** From the sixteenth century the Talmud was printed under Christian ecclesiastical supervision. The Basel edition of 1578–1581 was produced with expurgations; subsequent editions substituted terms — _kuti_, _akum_, _min_ — for the original readings, sometimes to avoid the censor, sometimes at the censor's insistence. Modern critical and restored editions have reinstated much of this material.

This matters for method. Contemporary polemicists frequently work from whichever edition supports the reading they want, and often from a Latin translation of a nineteenth-century German paraphrase of an early-modern Latin compendium. The claim to have "read the Talmud" almost never survives the question _which text, in which language, with which apparatus_.

## III. The Genealogy of the Anti-Talmud Corpus

Here the historical record becomes unusually clean, and it is the single most useful thing to know about this subject.

The list of incriminating Talmudic passages that circulates today is not the product of independent inquiry by many readers arriving separately at the same conclusions. It is a **transmitted text with a traceable lineage**, in which each generation copies its predecessor, including its predecessor's errors.

**1240: Paris.** Nicholas Donin, a Jewish convert to Christianity, presented thirty-five charges against the Talmud to Pope Gregory IX. The resulting disputation, in which Rabbi Yechiel of Paris defended the text, ended with the condemnation and public burning of cartloads of manuscripts in 1242. Donin's charge sheet is the ancestor of the modern list.

**1263 and 1413–14: Barcelona and Tortosa.** Further disputations, again organized by converts (Pablo Christiani; Joshua Halorki, later Jerónimo de Santa Fe), again working from selected passages, again with the burden of proof structurally reversed.

**1509–1520: The Pfefferkorn–Reuchlin affair.** Johannes Pfefferkorn, another convert, sought imperial authority to confiscate Jewish books. Johannes Reuchlin, the leading Christian Hebraist of the age, opposed him — and was himself put on trial for it. The episode is significant because Reuchlin's defense turned on precisely the philological point at issue: that Pfefferkorn could not read the texts he proposed to destroy.

**1700: Eisenmenger.** Johann Andreas Eisenmenger's _Entdecktes Judenthum_ is the pivot of the whole tradition. Two thick volumes, thousands of citations, an apparatus that looks scholarly. It is the source from which nearly all subsequent compendia are drawn. Its method — extraction without context, aggadic material read as legal doctrine, tendentious translation — became the template.

**1871: Rohling.** August Rohling, a Catholic priest and professor at Prague, published _Der Talmudjude_, a popularization of Eisenmenger. It went through many editions, was translated widely, and became the standard reference for antisemitic journalism across Central Europe. Rohling offered to testify under oath to the reality of Jewish ritual murder in connection with the Tiszaeszlár blood-libel case of 1882.

Rabbi Joseph Samuel Bloch responded in print, accusing Rohling of ignorance and forgery, and made a public offer: three thousand florins if Rohling could correctly translate a single randomly selected page of the Talmud. Rohling declined the test and sued Bloch for libel instead. Bloch's defense assembled the Orientalists Theodor Nöldeke and Karl August Wünsche, along with a compilation of verified translations of hundreds of the disputed passages. In 1885, shortly before trial, Rohling withdrew the suit, paid costs, and lost his chair.

**1892: Pranaitis.** Justinas Pranaitis, a Lithuanian priest teaching Hebrew in Saint Petersburg, published _Christianus in Talmude Iudaeorum_ — in English, _The Talmud Unmasked_. It draws on Rohling and Ecker, and through them on Eisenmenger. Pranaitis had limited Hebrew and could not read Aramaic, which is the language of most of the Gemara.

In 1913, at the Beilis blood-libel trial in Kiev, Pranaitis was produced as the prosecution's expert on Jewish hatred of Christians. The journalist and historian Ben-Zion Katz prepared a set of elementary questions for the defense. Pranaitis could not define _ḥullin_. He could not define _eruvin_. Asked what _Bava Batra_ was, with the question framed as though the words might be someone's name, he did not recognize the title of a tractate he had cited in his own book. The prosecution had retained, as its expert on the Talmud, a man who did not know that Bava Batra is part of the Talmud. The courtroom laughed. A Tsarist police observer reported internally that the cross-examination had gutted the evidentiary value of the testimony. Beilis was acquitted.

**The structural point.** Twice, in two countries, under two legal systems, the central authorities of this literature were placed under adversarial examination on the specific question of whether they could read the texts they claimed to expose. Both times they failed, publicly and catastrophically. Both times the failure had no effect whatever on the circulation of their books.

That last fact is the interesting one. A body of claims that survives the demonstrated incompetence of its sources is not functioning as a body of empirical claims. It is functioning as something else.

## IV. Reading the Passages Honestly

The genealogical argument is necessary but not sufficient. A corrupt transmission history does not prove that every underlying passage is benign. Some are not. Here is where I depart from the standard defense.

**The passages concerning Jesus.** The Babylonian Talmud contains a small number of hostile references to a figure identified with Jesus, and to a figure punished in the world to come. Peter Schäfer's _Jesus in the Talmud_ (Princeton, 2007) treats these seriously and, in my judgment, correctly: they are deliberate counter-narratives to the Gospel accounts, composed in Sasanian Babylonia, where Jewish scholars were free from Christian political power in a way their Palestinian colleagues were not. They are polemic — the literary self-assertion of a minority that had been comprehensively defeated in the argument that mattered, written in the one jurisdiction where such writing was safe.

Calling this polemic is not an apology for it. But it is a description of what it is: a scattering of counter-legends across a corpus in which they play no structural role, and from which no legal or practical consequence was ever derived.

**The passages concerning gentiles.** Several sugyot distinguish Jewish from non-Jewish parties in tort, property, and capital law. The categories in play — _goy_, _akum_, _oved kokhavim_, _nokhri_ — are not stable across the corpus, and the referent in most cases is the pagan of late antiquity, embedded in a cultic-legal order that the rabbis understood as fundamentally lawless.

What matters is what the tradition did with this material, and here the record is explicit rather than inferred:

- **Rabbi Menachem ha-Meiri** (Perpignan, 1249–1315), in his _Beit ha-Beḥirah_, developed the category of _umot ha-gedurot be-darkhei ha-datot_ — nations bounded by the ways of religion — and used it systematically to exclude contemporary Christians and Muslims from every halakhic disability that attached to ancient idolaters. This is not a marginal gloss; it is a sustained reinterpretive program, and Jacob Katz's _Exclusiveness and Tolerance_ (1961) remains the classic study of it.
- **Tosafot** (Sanhedrin 63b; Bekhorot 2b) held that gentiles are not warned against _shittuf_ — associating another with God — which functionally removed Trinitarian Christianity from the category of idolatry for Noahide purposes.
- **Maimonides**, _Hilkhot Melakhim_ 10:12, rules that the poor of the gentiles are to be supported, their sick visited, and their dead buried along with Israel's, _mipnei darkhei shalom_.
- **The _Shulchan Arukh_** prohibits _gezel akum_ — theft from a gentile — outright (Ḥoshen Mishpat 348:2, 359:1), and the commentarial tradition treats it as aggravated rather than mitigated, because it adds _ḥillul ha-Shem_.

The persistent claim that Jewish law licenses defrauding non-Jews is thus not a contested interpretation of an ambiguous source. It is a claim about the content of the codes that can be checked against the codes, and it is false there.

**The genuine difficulties.** I am not going to pretend the record is uniform. Maimonides in _Hilkhot Melakhim_ 8:10 writes that Moses was commanded to compel the world's acceptance of the Noahide laws — a coercive clause with no parallel in the surrounding halakhic literature, situated in a chapter about messianic-era royal authority, never operative in any Jewish polity, and read variously by Maimonides' own successors. It is a hard text. There are others. The Zohar contains material about non-Jewish souls that is difficult by any standard, and which later Hasidic thought, including _Tanya_ chapter 1, inherited and reworked in ways that remain uncomfortable.

The honest position is this: the corpus is enormous, ancient, produced under conditions of subjugation and periodic massacre, and it contains material that reads badly now — as does Roman law, canon law, the _fiqh_, the Confucian legal tradition, and every other pre-modern normative literature that survived long enough to be embarrassed by its own longevity. The question that distinguishes traditions is not whether such material exists but what the tradition subsequently did with it. On that question the Jewish record is a fifteen-hundred-year history of narrowing, reinterpretation, and functional abandonment, conducted in public, in writing, and available for inspection.

## V. What Kind of Object Chabad Is

Chabad-Lubavitch is a school of Hasidism founded by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (1745–1812), whose _Tanya_ (_Likkutei Amarim_, 1797) is among the most systematic works of Jewish mystical psychology ever written. The acronym — _Ḥokhmah, Binah, Da'at_ — announces the movement's distinguishing commitment: that mystical apprehension should proceed through sustained intellectual labor rather than around it. This set Chabad apart from the more affectively oriented Hasidic courts, including my own.

Two historical facts about the movement's origins are worth noting, because they invert the usual picture.

First, Hasidism was itself the object of ferocious internal Jewish opposition. The Vilna Gaon issued bans; the _mitnagdim_ denounced the new movement as heretical. Shneur Zalman was denounced to the Tsarist authorities by fellow Jews and imprisoned in the Peter and Paul Fortress in 1798. The nineteenth of Kislev, celebrated by Chabad as its festival of liberation, commemorates his release. The movement began as a persecuted minority within a persecuted minority.

Second, the contemporary global Chabad is largely the creation of one man in one generation. Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (1902–1994), the seventh rebbe, inherited a movement devastated by the Holocaust and Soviet repression and rebuilt it around _shliḥut_ — the dispatch of emissary couples to establish permanent institutions in places with negligible Jewish infrastructure. There are now several thousand such couples.

This last point explains a great deal about why Chabad in particular attracts conspiratorial attention. The movement is _architecturally visible_. It is centralized, identifiable, present in a great many countries, deliberately public, and organized around a single revered figure and a single address. To a mode of thought that reads structure as evidence of conspiracy, an organization designed to be findable will always look like an organization that has been found out. Sue Fishkoff's _The Rebbe's Army_ (2003) is a useful non-polemical account of how the network actually operates; Naftali Loewenthal's _Communicating the Infinite_ (1990) covers the intellectual history.

This also lets me return to the one genuinely hard Chabad text, which I flagged earlier and do not want to leave hanging. _Tanya_'s opening chapter locates the vitalizing soul of the nations in the three wholly impure _kelipot_, and read as ontology it says something ugly about the worth of non-Jewish souls. The words support the reading, and I am not going to dissolve them on the page. But a doctrine is tested by what its holders do with it, and the man who inherited that chapter spent the last decades of his life on a global campaign to teach gentiles that they hold a share in the world to come and a divine mandate of their own. One does not campaign for the spiritual dignity of beings one takes to be worthless. The Noahide program treated in the next section is not incidental to Chabad; it is the movement's own gloss on its first chapter, and it narrows the text exactly as the Meiri narrowed the passages on the ancient idolater - not by denying the words but by declining to build a world on them.

## VI. The Noahide Claim

The most specific charge against Chabad concerns the Noahide laws, and it deserves a specific answer.

**The doctrine.** The Talmud (Sanhedrin 56a–60a) sets out seven commandments understood to bind all humanity: prohibitions of idolatry, blasphemy, murder, sexual immorality, theft, and eating flesh torn from a living animal, together with a positive obligation to establish courts. Maimonides codifies this in _Hilkhot Melakhim_ 8–10.

**What the doctrine asserts.** It is essential to see what work this framework does in Jewish thought. It is the mechanism by which Judaism holds that _non-Jews need not become Jews_. A gentile who observes these commandments is a _ḥasid umot ha-olam_ with a share in the world to come (_Hilkhot Melakhim_ 8:11). Judaism does not proselytize, has no soteriological monopoly, and has never held that salvation requires membership. The Noahide framework is the doctrinal source of that restraint.

To read it as a program for governing gentiles is to read it exactly backwards. Its function in the system is to make a universal claim about human moral obligation while explicitly declining a universal claim about religious membership.

**The legislative claim.** The recurring assertion is that the United States has already enacted the Noahide code, through Public Law 102-14 (H.J. Res. 104, 20 March 1991).

The document is short and public. It is a joint resolution designating 26 March 1991 as "Education Day, U.S.A." in honor of Schneerson's eighty-ninth birthday. Its preamble includes a _whereas_ clause describing ethical values that "have been the bedrock of society from the dawn of civilization, when they were known as the Seven Noahide Laws." Its operative clause — the portion after _Resolved_ — designates the day and requests that the President issue a proclamation.

That is the entire legal content. A _whereas_ clause is recital, not enactment; it creates no rights, duties, jurisdiction, or penalties, and courts do not treat preambular language as operative. Commemorative resolutions of this type were being passed by the hundreds annually in that era. The statute establishes no courts, defines no offenses, and prescribes no punishments, because a commemorative resolution is structurally incapable of doing any of those things.

The claim that this law authorizes capital punishment of non-Jews is not a strained reading of an ambiguous text. It is an assertion about the contents of a two-page public document that anyone can read in about ninety seconds.

## VII. Messianism, and Why the Controversy Is the Answer

Chabad messianism is a real phenomenon and a real problem, and the way to address it is not to minimize it.

In the final years of Schneerson's life, a substantial current within the movement identified him as the Messiah. After his death in 1994, some continued to do so, and a minority maintains positions ranging from expectation of his return to claims that shade into the theologically extraordinary. The movement did not appoint an eighth rebbe.

The most rigorous critique of this development was written not by an outside investigator but by an Orthodox Jewish historian: David Berger's _The Rebbe, the Messiah, and the Scandal of Orthodox Indifference_ (2001). Berger's argument is that messianist Chabad has crossed a genuine doctrinal line and that the wider Orthodox world's failure to say so is itself a scandal. Rabbi Elazar Menachem Shach, among the most authoritative _Litvish_ figures of the late twentieth century, attacked Chabad institutionally and in the harshest theological terms available to him.

Now observe what this does to the conspiratorial frame. The frame requires Jewish unity — a coordinated, doctrinally uniform collective pursuing a common program. What the record shows instead is a bitter, public, decades-long intra-Jewish theological war, conducted in print, in Hebrew and English, by named parties, over precisely the question of whether one Hasidic movement has departed from normative Judaism.

This is not the archaeology of a conspiracy. It is the ordinary sociology of religion. The material that would be needed to establish coordination is the same material that documents the schism.

## VIII. The Structure of the Argument Itself

Set the particulars aside and look at the argumentative form, because the form is what makes the enterprise self-sustaining.

**Selection without a denominator.** The method produces a list of passages. It never produces the ratio. From a corpus of 2,700 folios one can assemble thirty damning quotations, and thirty out of 2,700 is a rate that any large text will meet on any theme one cares to name. Without a denominator, the list is uninterpretable — and the denominator is never supplied, because supplying it would end the argument.

**The closed citation loop.** As shown above, the sources cite each other. Pranaitis cites Rohling and Ecker; Rohling reproduces Eisenmenger; Eisenmenger inherits the medieval disputation charge sheets. Independent confirmation is simulated by repetition. This is why apparent convergence across many books proves nothing: they are not many observations but one observation copied many times.

**The esoteric double bind.** The claim that the doctrine is secret makes disconfirmation impossible in principle. If the texts say something benign, that is the exoteric surface. If a Jewish reader denies the charge, the denial is what the doctrine instructs. A hypothesis that assigns confirming weight to both the evidence and its absence has left the domain of empirical claims entirely.

The relevant test — and I have argued this at length elsewhere in a different context — is **counterfactual**: what observation, if made, would the proponent accept as disconfirming? Where the answer is _none_, the disagreement is not about evidence and cannot be resolved by producing more of it. This is the reason I do not think debating in such threads is useful, and it is not a claim about the sincerity of any individual.

**Fabricated primary sources.** The article that prompted this essay cites the "Constantinople Letter of 1489," presented as a directive from a Jewish Sanhedrin instructing Spanish Jews to convert nominally and subvert Christian society from within.

The Sanhedrin did not exist in 1489. The institution ended in late antiquity; there was no Jewish high court sitting at Constantinople or anywhere else. No Hebrew, Aramaic, Ladino, or Judeo-Arabic original of the letter has ever been produced. Its earliest appearance is in _La Silva Curiosa_ by Julio Íñiguez de Medrano — a Spanish miscellany of curiosities and entertainments — introduced with the frame that it was discovered in the archives of Toledo by a hermit of Salamanca, which is a literary device rather than a provenance. Its surviving versions disagree on whether the persecuting monarch is the King of Spain or the King of France, and on the dates. It reached its modern audience through the same channel as the _Protocols_.

**Sources that are known forgeries.** The _Protocols_ were exposed as a plagiarism of Maurice Joly's _Dialogue aux Enfers entre Machiavel et Montesquieu_ (1864) by Philip Graves in _The Times_ in August 1921, and adjudicated a forgery in Bern in 1935. Norman Cohn's _Warrant for Genocide_ (1967) is the standard history. A bibliography that recommends the _Protocols_ is not a bibliography with one weak entry; it is a bibliography whose evidentiary standard has been publicly established.

**The symmetry test.** Finally, apply the method to itself. Take any large religious or legal corpus with a two-thousand-year history — canon law, Roman law, the _fiqh_, the patristic literature, the Lutheran corpus — and apply Eisenmenger's procedure: extract without context, treat rejected and rhetorical material as doctrine, read historical polemic as standing policy, ignore the interpretive tradition. Every one of them yields a monstrous result. A method that returns a conviction regardless of which defendant is placed before it is not detecting anything about the defendant.

I note without further comment that Luther's _On the Jews and Their Lies_ appears on the article's reading list as evidence about Jews, rather than as a specimen of the genre under study.

## IX. What Is Actually at Stake

I have tried to write this as an examination of method rather than a defense, because I think the methodological point is the durable one and the defensive posture is a trap. A tradition that can only be defended by being declared spotless will eventually be indefensible, since no tradition of any age is spotless.

The Talmud is a record of argument. It contains polemic produced under Roman and Sasanian rule by a community that had lost its temple, its polity, and a great many of its people. It contains material that reads badly, and it contains fifteen centuries of subsequent argument about what to do with that material, most of it in the direction of narrowing. Chabad is a two-hundred-year-old intellectual school within Hasidism, itself once denounced as heresy by other Jews, now organized around a global outreach network, and currently in the middle of a public theological crisis about its own late leader that other Orthodox Jews have been documenting and attacking in print for three decades.

Neither is a secret. Both are among the most extensively published, translated, digitized, and commented-upon bodies of material in the history of religion. The _Shulchan Arukh_ is online. The Talmud is online, in Hebrew and Aramaic with English translation and cross-referenced commentary, free, at Sefaria. The _Tanya_ is online. Public Law 102-14 is on the Government Publishing Office's website.

The concealment thesis requires that a tradition which has spent two millennia writing everything down, printing it, arguing about it in public, and putting it on the internet is nonetheless hiding its actual doctrine. That thesis is not defeated by any particular counter-quotation. It is defeated by the observation that it was never the kind of thing evidence could touch.

I remain willing to learn any specific page with anyone who names one.
