# Every Nation Is Chosen

*On Jewish supremacy and the narcissism of nations*

*My Big Fat Greek Wedding (2002). Directed by Joel Zwick. Starring Nia Vardalos, John Corbett, Michael Constantine. Gus Portokalos can prove that every word in every language comes from a Greek root, he heals every wound with Windex, and he divides humanity in two: Greeks, and everyone who wishes they were Greek. The film doesn't mock him - it loves him. Because in the right dose, this national narcissism isn't a disease. It's home.*

There is an accusation going around everywhere now - in podcasts, on social media, at the edges of academia: "Jewish supremacy." Judaism, they say, is a religion of arrogance. A people that calls itself "the chosen people" - what is that if not collective narcissism? And unlike other accusations, this one has a text. "You have chosen us from among all the nations." That is not a hostile quote ripped out of context. It's from the siddur. A Jew says it three times a day.

So let's give the accusers what they are owed: yes, Judaism has a claim of election. It is not marginal, it is not a metaphor, and whoever tries to make it disappear is guilty of apologetics. The question is not whether the claim exists. The question is what exactly it claims - and whether there is a single nation in the world that doesn't claim it.

## Who else is chosen?

Let's take a tour. (A partial one, for reasons of space - the full list is waiting in the appendix at the end.)

The Afrikaners in South Africa understood their victory at the Battle of Blood River in 1838 as a covenant with God: they were a chosen people, with a divine mandate over South Africa. That theology carried apartheid on its back. The Polish Romantics, with the poet Mickiewicz at their head, declared Poland "the Christ of nations" - crucified in the partitions, destined to redeem Europe through its suffering. The Serbs carry the Kosovo myth: Prince Lazar chose the heavenly kingdom over the earthly one, and so the Serbs are a "heavenly people." The Ethiopians, in the Kebra Nagast, claim descent from Solomon - and that the Ark of the Covenant itself is with them, in Axum. That is: the election passed to them, physically, with the Ark.

And the great religions? Christianity is built on supersessionism: the Church is the "true Israel," the new chosen people, and outside the Church there is no salvation. Islam declares in the Quran: "You are the best community brought forth for mankind" - and all previous revelations? Corrupted. Only ours was preserved. The Mormons moved the covenant to America, and every convert is adopted, literally, into the people of Israel.

Notice the irony: a good share of the nations and religions that accuse Judaism over its chosenness claim - claim Judaism's chosenness claim, for themselves. The claim is pathological, narcissistic, insufferable - and also, it's ours now.

## And who is at the center?

And there are those who didn't wait for theology. China calls itself the Middle Kingdom - the land of the center, the center of the world - the emperor was the Son of Heaven, and all other peoples were barbarians, graded by their distance from Chinese civilization. The Greeks divided humanity into Greeks and barbarians - "barbarians" because their language sounds like noise, "bar-bar." Pericles called Athens "the school of Hellas." Rome saw itself as bringing law and order to a wild world. The Sanskrit sources divide the world into Aryans and the impure, and India is the only land where liberation is possible at all. And Persia - the name "Iran" literally means "land of the Aryans." The noble ones.

In the modern era it just changed clothes. Germany: "The German essence shall heal the world" - a line by the poet Geibel that became a slogan. France: the "civilizing mission," and the belief - alive and kicking to this day - that French values simply are the universal values. Notice that this is a more sophisticated narcissism than all the others: we are not special, we are just right on everyone's behalf. Britain: "the white man's burden," and an empire on which the sun never sets, under the wing of Providence. Russia is especially rich: Moscow is the "Third Rome" ("and a fourth there shall not be"), and the Russian people, in Dostoevsky, is "the God-bearing nation," destined to save the decadent West. America: "a city upon a hill," Manifest Destiny, "the indispensable nation." And Japan, which sounds modest to us: the imperial line descends directly from the sun goddess, Japan is "the land of the gods" - and the idea survived 1945 in secular form, an entire popular literary genre devoted to proving that the Japanese language, the Japanese brain, and even the Japanese intestines - are unique among humankind.

The intestines.

## The human beings

And if this sounds to you like a disease of empires, here is its oldest and simplest version. An enormous number of peoples - the Inuit, the Navajo, the Roma, the Ainu, the Bantu - call themselves, in their own language, simply "the human beings." That's the name. The Khoikhoi of southern Africa call themselves "men of men." Think for a moment about the claim built into that: whoever is not one of us - is not entirely human. That is a more radical claim than election. Because election, at least, presupposes that the non-chosen are human too.

It turns out this is not a Jewish invention at all. It is the default setting of human group psychology. There is already an empirical literature on this - researchers measure "collective narcissism" as a trait found among nations, religions, political parties, and even football fans.

So let's grant the accusers' premise, in full: calling yourself chosen is narcissism. Granted. But then every civilization in history is narcissistic, and the diagnosis has lost all content - it's like diagnosing someone with having a pulse. And if, out of all these nations, only the Jewish version counts as pathology, the question becomes: why this one exactly? And the answer to that question usually tells you more about the accuser than about the accused.

## And maybe they're actually right?

But I want to go one step further, because "everyone does it" is still not "and it's fine." The step is this: national narcissism is almost always an exaggeration of something real.

The Greeks really were the school of the world - we still sit in their classrooms, we call them "logic" and "democracy." Rome really gave the West its law. China really was the center of an entire civilization for over a thousand years. The French really formulated ideas that spread across the whole world. The Germany of "the German essence" is also the Germany of Bach and Kant. And Dostoevsky, who believed the Russian people would save the world, wrote the deepest novels there are about the human soul. Each nation's arrogance is organized around a real excellence of its own. The narcissism doesn't invent the diamond. It only forgets that there are other diamonds.

And this is true at the level of the individual too. A person needs a certain dose of narcissism just to get out of bed in the morning. The sense of worth - "I am worth something, what I do matters" - is not a character flaw. It is a precondition for character. A person who is sure he is special can turn into a monster of entitlement: "I deserve it." But the very same thing can produce the exact opposite: someone who was raised on "you are the son of a king" and understands it correctly does not permit himself what he permits others. Nobility, at its best, is not a privilege. It is a code of conduct. Everything depends on what the specialness demands of you.

And this is exactly where the line runs between kinds of chosenness.

## Liability, not privilege

Almost every version of election we've surveyed confers something: a mandate over the land, rule over the barbarians, an empire under God's wing, exclusive salvation. The election entitles. Now listen to the founding formulation of Jewish election, in Amos: "You only have I known of all the families of the earth - **therefore** I will punish you for all your iniquities."

Therefore. Only you have I known, and therefore I will punish you. Not "therefore the land is yours," not "therefore you shall rule." Therefore I will be exacting with you over every sin. This is an election whose entire content is heightened responsibility: the chosen carry 613 commandments, the rest of the world - seven. And salvation, the grand prize? The sages ruled: the righteous people of **all** nations have a share in the world to come. Jewish election may be the only one on the list that doesn't even confer a monopoly on heaven. And in that same book of Amos, when Israel gets puffed up anyway, the prophet flattens the pretension in a single sentence: "Are you not like the children of the Cushites to me, O children of Israel?"

And it starts even earlier, in the first election itself. When God chooses Abraham, the declared purpose is not Abraham: "And in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed." From the very first moment, the election is formulated as a job toward everyone else. Not a lottery win - a position. With everything a position entails: demands, reviews, and the possibility of temporary dismissal, with exile.

And there is one more piece of evidence, maybe the strongest of all, and it sits on the shelf of every synagogue: Israel is the only nation that preserved, in its own holy canon, its own prophets calling it stiff-necked. Open the Bible at random - there is a decent chance you'll land on a rebuke. No nation writes its national epic like that. Our national epic is the file of complaints against us, and we kissed it, placed it in a gilded ark, and danced with it.

Tevye the milkman said all of it in one line, lifting his eyes to heaven: "I know we are the chosen people. But once in a while, can't You choose someone else?"

## So what's left of the accusation?

What's left is this: every nation is sure it is special, and every nation is also a little right. National specialness is like a child's specialness in a family - real, precious, and dangerous only when it turns from "I owe because of it" into "I deserve because of it." The Jews are not the only nation that thinks it was chosen. Judaism may be the only place where the chosenness is formulated, in black and white, as a debt and not a right - and where the nation keeping that formulation has been paying for it for thousands of years, and still won't give it up.

So the next time someone says "Jewish supremacy" to you, you can quietly ask: which nation in history hasn't claimed supremacy? And what would yours have been willing to pay for it?
