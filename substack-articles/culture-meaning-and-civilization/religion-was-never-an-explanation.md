# Religion Was Never an Explanation

*Fiddler on the Roof (1971). Directed by Norman Jewison. Starring Topol, Norma Crane, Leonard Frey. Tevye the dairyman argues with God all day long, out loud, on a first-name basis, the way you argue with a partner you cannot divorce. He never once tries to prove God exists. That was settled long before the film began. What is left is the conversation inside the covenant, and the tradition that holds the terms.*

For a few hundred years now, science has been winning a fight that never happened.

The story everyone learned goes like this. Once, religion explained the world. Lightning was God's anger, plague was God's judgment, the sun crossed the sky because that was the route God set for it. Then science arrived with better explanations and took the territory back, one field at a time, until religion was left holding only the gaps, the shrinking set of things we cannot yet account for. God of the gaps. And the gaps keep closing.

It is a clean story. It is also a category error in the first sentence.

## Religion doesn't explain. It asserts.

Done honestly, a religion is not in the explanation business at all. Take the one I know. The Jewish claim is not "here is a mechanism for lightning." It is a contract. We were slaves in Egypt. Something took us out, in a way the people standing there could not deny, and made an offer on the way out: I saved you, you are now my people, here are the terms. That is not a hypothesis competing with thermodynamics. It is testimony, and a deal struck on the strength of it.

You cannot beat an assertion with a better explanation, because they are not the same kind of move. It is like answering a marriage with a weather report. When a physicist explains how the eye evolved, he has not refuted a covenant any more than a better map refutes a promise. The two were never reaching for the same thing. One says how the machinery runs. The other says who you belong to and what you owe. Science can be completely correct and leave the contract entirely untouched, because the contract never made a claim about the machinery.

So when people say "science disproved religion," listen to what is actually being said: a tool that measures mechanism found no mechanism where the believer was not pointing at mechanism in the first place.

## You don't re-prove a diploma

The honest objection comes back: fine, but why should anyone accept the testimony now, thousands of years downstream?

Because that is not how established things work, for anyone. A covenant gets founded once, on what a generation witnessed, and then it gets carried, not re-derived every morning. This is why the tradition is built almost entirely out of memory: the commandments to remember the exodus, to retell it, to eat and rest and mark time around it. The point is not nostalgia. The point is that the founding event is the evidence, and the remembering is how a founding event stays load-bearing across centuries. After you graduate you do not sit the entrance exam again every time someone asks whether you can read.

And it is not the unfalsifiable hand-waving its critics assume, either. It stakes claims in history. Prophecy is a bet that can lose, a statement about what will happen in time, exposed to time. A faith that says "this will come to pass" has put something on the table that the world can knock over. That is the opposite of a claim engineered to be safe.

## So who, exactly, did science beat?

No one. Science measured against its own reasoning is one of the most magnificent things our species has done, and it stands on its own without needing a defeated rival to stand on. It simply never had a religious opponent in the ring, because religion was not offering a competing mechanism to be outperformed. "God of the gaps" lands on a strawman the serious believer never held. The believer was not using God to plug holes in physics. He was standing in a covenant and watching physics do its excellent work inside a world he reads as given.

And notice the bill the confident atheist still has not paid. Why is there anything at all? Every explanation leans on a prior explanation, and the chain cannot run backward forever; it has to stop at something that itself needs no explanation. The materialist walks up to that wall and calls it a brute fact. The believer walks up to the same wall and calls it God. Same wall. The argument was never about whether there is a wall. It is about whether you let yourself feel that you are standing in front of one.

And philosophy cannot settle this either, not because it isn't sharp, but because of how it forces you to argue.

*Draft seed, expand:* Try to argue against physicalism. You will find there is no "physicalism" to aim at. There is the physicalism that holds physics is already complete and the one that grants physics may still change out from under us; the physicalist who keeps free will and the one who throws it out; reductive, non-reductive, emergentist, eliminativist, and every combination of the above. To land a single clean claim you are expected to account for all of it, every branch and every cross of branches, and if you don't, a philosopher will tell you, correctly, that you have not touched his particular version. He is right. The precision is real, and being precise is a virtue. But past a point the virtue inverts. A position carved into enough qualified variants becomes a thing you can never finish arguing against, because the instant you aim, it has already shifted to a version you didn't name. You are not refuted, you are deferred, forever, on grounds of incompleteness. The result is that the proliferation does not sharpen the question; it makes any simple true thing nearly impossible to say out loud without a hundred footnotes, and a claim that needs a hundred footnotes to survive is a claim no one can actually use. *Develop: the physics-can-change move, the free-will split, reductive vs non-reductive; "being overly precise makes it difficult to claim anything"; and why this leaves the religion question unsettled by philosophy in either direction. ## [STUB - to develop] Accept first, understand later

There is a deeper difference underneath the method, and it decides what each one can ever reach.

*Draft seed, expand:* Philosophy tests. An idea has to justify itself to your reason at the door, and whatever cannot make sense to you right now does not get in. Spirituality runs the order backwards. You accept the teaching first, often before it makes any sense, sometimes when it looks plainly backwards, and you live inside it until the depth opens, which it does only after you have stopped demanding that it prove itself in advance. Do first, understand later. The catch the testing method never notices is what its filter throws out: the ideas that fail the entrance exam are exactly the ones sitting above your current understanding, because those are the ones that cannot yet make sense to you. A method that only admits what you can already validate can never carry you past your own ceiling. Authoritative wisdom can, and that is the whole point of its being authoritative: you trust it through the stretch where you do not yet understand, so it can take you somewhere reason would not have approved the way to. That is the real line between a wisdom with no authority and a wisdom with one. One confirms where you already stand. The other moves you. *Develop: naaseh v'nishma (do, then hear); the entrance-exam filter; tie to faith-as-incubation and "going through the motions brings faith"; why testing-first guarantees you stay at your present depth.*

## You argue with God, not for Him

Which brings us back to Tevye, who has the posture exactly right and never reads a word of any of this.

He does not reason his way to God and he does not defend Him like a theorem under attack. He argues with Him, constantly, on the road, in the barn, in the middle of his own ruin: a running complaint addressed to the second party of a contract he has no intention of leaving. That is what belief looks like from the inside when it is honest. Not "I have a better explanation than yours." Something closer to "I was there, we made a deal, and I am still in it, and I have some things to say to You about how it's going."

Science never threatened that, because science was never speaking to it. The man who thinks he chose evidence over faith has usually just chosen a different thing to stand in front of without admitting he is standing in front of anything at all.
