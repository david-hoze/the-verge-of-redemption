# Enemy deciphered

*An interpretation*

The film opens with Saramago's epigraph — *chaos is order yet undeciphered*. That's the key to the movie. What you see as chaos, will become order once you decipher it.

The movie is weird. It seems at first as though there are plot holes, and the movie is forcefully trying to be eerie. Adam is acting really strange, and is over excited about someone that looks like him. A normal person would just be surprised, maybe confused, but not as excited. he calls Anthony's home like a weirdo, scaring his wife. And Anthony's wife goes searching for Adam. She finds him at the university, and is shocked. Adam greets her like a stranger, and she's just shocked to her senses. a wife that sees a double of her husband would be surprised, amazed, awe-stricken, but she seems *shocked*. When she asks Anthony about it, he says he doesn't know what she's talking about, and she says "I think you know, I think you know", and cries. Why does she cry? At the last scenes, Adam is taking Anthony's place, and Helen isn't surprised, and acts as if everything is normal. She asks "How was school?". Adam works at a school. We don't even know what's Anthony's job. They never talk about it, and he has a pretty luxurious house, and had apparently leaved his third rate acting career, which didn't seem profitable at all.

Also, Adam discovers his cut out photo of himself, inside Anthony's home, together with *Helen*. What? How can that be? And how come they have the same scar? And why does Adam's mother mention his fantasies of becoming a third rate actor? Anthony is the actor. She also tells him to eat Blueberries. But Anthony is the one that has blueberries in his shake, and Adam says he doesn't like them. What's going on? Are they twins? So why do they have the same scar? Does he have a split personality? Split personalities don't treat the other one as a double. And what's with all the spiders?

The movie answers it casually in the first lecture. It's all about control. First dictatorships controlled by force, or bread and circuses. Now they control via information control. Censorship, misinformation, lies. Anthony and Adam are the same person. A history professor that is drawn to acting, and even had a go at it. Anthony left his acting career, and created a new identity, Adam Bell. Anthony has a cheating problem. When "Adam" first called, Helen immediately suspected, "Are you seeing her again?". Anthony is an actor. He's a history professor. He decided to create the perfect alibi, perfect cover story. There's a double. A man named Adam Bell, that looks exactly like him. It's a mystery. Even if you check under the blouse, he has the same scar. If Helen, or Mary, ever catch him with one another, it was the double. He's not cheating.

But wait, what's going on? We are shown Adam and Anthony in a scene where they're *together*. They're *not* the same person. Well, this is the point of the whole film. It's a story. We're being told a story. No one ever sees Anthony and Adam in the same room. The encounters are what he concocts in his mind, what he's planning to tell Helen or Mary if they ever found out. And we are given the lie. Besides the weird hints we got, the torn picture, Adam's fantasies of an acting career, the fact that Anthony's job is never known, the two identities are rigorously kept throughout the movie, including different personalities. You can always think that Anthony and Adam are not the same person. This is control. The perfect alibi, the perfect cover story.

But Helen doesn't buy it. That's why she's in shock. That's why she's crying. That's why she's saying cryptically "I think you know". That's why she's facing "Adam's" shyness and different personality as normal. She knows it's Anthony. She knows it's her husband. But he never gives up the show, he never tells. And we are shown what Helen is shown, or potentially shown. We are Helen, trying to decipher the order out of the chaos, the truth behind the lies.

Actually we are not exactly Helen, we are less knowledgeable, because we don't know what Anthony's job is. Even when Helen says "How was school?", maybe she was talking to Adam? Maybe she likes him better? Maybe she wants him instead of Anthony? It's highly unlikely, why would she want Adam instead of her husband, a stranger she just met. But the movie leaves it open, that's the mechanism. That's the control Anthony created, and the film always keeps the option of two doubles existing open.

And what about the spider? Villeneuve has connected the imagery to Louise Bourgeois's _Maman_ sculptures, where the spider is the mother: protective and monstrous at the same time. Nourishing and devouring. He openly said that the movie is about fear of commitment. Anthony sees his wife as the **Enemy**, the spider that threatens to devour him. He goes to the secret erotic club meetings, where the stripper squashes a tarantula. The club is arranged to crush the wife, the devouring creature.

Well, all of this stands, but what about the switch? The blackmailing? Why would Anthony plan a night for Adam and Helen, and a night for "him" and Mary? Anthony wants to kill Mary. He wants to get rid of her. He knows Helen is on to him, but he never confesses. He never will. He comes home as Adam one day. An impersonator. It's before the crash. Now Helen is the cheater. She's cheating with Adam, and he can always point that out to her. Even if she says "I didn't know", he would say "you did, and you still did it, I know". That's his method. Lying, and asserting the lie.

Wait, what about the car crash? Supposedly, Adam having sex with Helen, and Anthony crashing with Mary happened at the same time! Well, that's just intermixed scenes, it doesn't mean they happened at the same time. They can be one person, and the crash happened *after* the night of "Adam" and Helen. And, more importantly, the final scene of the intermixed scenes is the *crash scene*. Right after that there's "Adam's" morning with Helen, where the radio reports an accident, and Adam switches the station. The news might say a man named Anthony Claire survived, while a woman named Mary didn't. He doesn't want Helen to know. Censorship.

But how did he survive the crash without a scratch? Well, that's a debt the movie has to own, because it has to tell us a story that will always keep us wondering whether they are two people or the same one. If he shows up with a scratch the next morning, then we know. for sure. He's lying. the movie wants to keep the lie perfect. That's the point, control. You control the information, thus you control the story, and the people. The hints are always just hints. You can always suppose a fantastic story, and the not a web of lies. these are mechanisms for us, the audience. We are lied to.

And we have to face the question, what is the truth? Do we believe Anthony/Adam's acting? The depressed history teacher, and the energetic third rate actor has been? The encounters with the double are the stories Anthony/Adam tells us. The scene with the other staff member recommending about the movie Adam supposedly found Anthony is, is just another lie. but is it possible to have an exact copy with the same scar? True, as an audience used to sci-fi films, we can think of a possible explanation, and that's one of the film's ways of keeping us in the question. But I think the goal of the film is to make us realize that Anthony/Adam is lying. He's a history teacher, well aware of how to control people through information. he lectures on it. He's a wannabe actor, and uses his acting skill to control the story he wants to sell. The only differentiator between Anthony and Adam is the mood, and Anthony/Adam uses it well.

Helen doesn't buy it. She knows her husband. She knows it's him throughout, and when she sees the double she's shocked. When he comes home talking about the blueberries nonchalantly, she knows he's lying, and she cries. She's married to a liar, pregnant, stuck. And he continues to compulsively lie. He even goes to his mother to corroborate the story. His mother accepts the premise, and now whenever Helen will talk to her, she'll mention the weird double. His mother is the one making him eat blueberries. she's the one forcing him to stay with his wife, give up the acting career. She's the enemy. the mother is the enemy, Helen and his mom. She's the arachnid. They both are.

The second lecture is about history repeating itself. At first it's a tragedy. Anthony/Adam is lying, deceiving his wife, killing his mistress, caught in a web of lies. but the pattern repeats. He went back to the acting agency, and got another invitation to the erotic club. That's not Adam smiling at the key he just got to some place he doesn't know. It's Anthony/Adam, repeating the same pattern, lying to his wife that he needs to "get out". Possibly finding another mistress in that club. And in the end, he sees his enemy. A giant spider. a lethal spider, cowering away in the corner of the room. Knowing it's going to be betrayed. Never seen as the loving wife. Just as an enemy.
