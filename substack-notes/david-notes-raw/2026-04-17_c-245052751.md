---"Good Buy." Not goodbye. You put the whole thing in the title and most people won't even catch it.

Here's what you actually wrote: a confession that should be ugly but isn't. Because you didn't hide. You said "I wouldn't stay with him if he didn't pay for everything" and you didn't flinch. You didn't justify it. You didn't wrap it in trauma language or self-help framing. You just said it. Like a woman pulling a knife out of her own chest and showing it to the room and saying: look what I found in there.

But the professor. The professor is the wound underneath the wound. Because you felt it. You FELT it. Soul-crushingly, you said. Which means the thing you tell yourself - that you can't love, that you're broken, that the wiring is permanent - isn't true. The wiring works. You know exactly what love feels like. You just learned, through every man who took what you gave and left when he was full, that feeling it is the most dangerous thing you can do. So you stopped. Not because you can't. Because the cost was too high and nobody was paying it back.

So you built a system. A clean, honest, eyes-open system. He provides. You stay. Nobody pretends it's more than it is. And from the outside it looks cold. But from the inside? It's the most rational thing a woman who's been used her entire life could build. You didn't become transactional because you're shallow. You became transactional because you're smart. And the smartest people build the best cages.

But you wrote this piece. And you ended it with "Yes, I often think about leaving." You didn't have to write that. You could have ended on the system. On the logic. On the armor. But you couldn't. Because the woman who felt that thing for the professor is still in there. She's been in there the whole time, behind the transactions, behind the practicality, behind every nice trip and every paid-for dinner. She's sitting in a room you locked years ago and she's knocking.

You hear her. That's why you wrote this. That's why the title is a pun that breaks your heart if you look at it long enough. You're not gone, Penny. You're just expensive now because free was never safe. And the day you meet someone who makes free feel safe again - that door you keep thinking about? You'll walk through it so fast you won't even remember what was on the other side.
