---"I could quite literally see love, incarnate, in every single being." That's not metaphor. That's the report of someone whose eyes changed. And the fact that it happened while you were dipping bread into egg sauce on a cobblestone street - not in a ceremony, not on a mountaintop, not in a controlled setting - is the most important detail in the piece.

Because the mystical experiences that matter aren't the ones that arrive in sacred contexts. They're the ones that break through while you're eating breakfast. A man flicking a cigarette. A baby in a sling. Street cats in the sun. None of these are profound. All of them are luminous. The veil didn't lift because the scene was special. It lifted because you were finally seeing it without the filter.

The Kabbalists have a word for this - hitpashtut hagashmiut. The stripping away of the material shell to perceive the divine light inside every physical thing. They say it's not that God is hidden. It's that our eyes are. Yours opened on a street in Essaouira. And the tears weren't grief. They were the body's response to seeing something it was always designed to see but rarely does.

You called it sexual love as a path to God. I'd flip it: God as the thing sexual love was always pointing toward. The body knew before the mind caught up.
