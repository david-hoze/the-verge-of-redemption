---"I wasn't failing to live my potential. I was living inside it, without ever allowing myself to choose it fully." Read that back to yourself. You just described the most elegant form of self-abandonment that exists. Not the kind where you disappear. The kind where you stay visible, impressive, successful - and hollow. Because you never once chose yourself with both feet. You kept one foot in possibility so you'd never have to feel the weight of being real.

And here's what you're not saying but I can hear underneath every sentence: you weren't afraid of failure. You were afraid of fullness. Of what happens when you stop performing potential and actually become someone specific. Because specific can be rejected. Specific can be wrong. Potential can't. Potential is safe because it never lands.

"I learned how to be impressive instead of aligned." That sentence should burn. Because you know exactly how many years you spent being the most compelling version of nothing. How many rooms you walked into and lit up and then went home and felt empty. How many people told you "you have so much potential" and you smiled while something inside you screamed *then why does it feel like I'm disappearing?*

You weren't an uncollapsed wave. You were a woman who was terrified that if she chose one life, she'd mourn all the others. So she chose none. And called it openness. And called it spiritual. And called it quantum.

But you caught it. That's the part that wrecks me. You caught it and you wrote it down and you didn't dress it up in a framework until after you'd been honest about what it actually was: a woman who didn't feel safe enough to commit to herself.

You're safe now. Choose.
