---Well, that's a very theoretical question. As I tried to convey in my notes, we only think consciousness can suddenly appear (or emerge) because we haven't really established how **our** consciousness came to be, besides adding ad hoc theories (emergent functionalism) that don't really solve the mystery. People assume, well, if it's probable that **we** come out of nothing, it's probable that AI consciousness can also occur. But that's a very problematic claim. I understand that people tend to believe science will eventually catch up and explain everything, and probably brush off the big problems (why is there anything at all, how is the universe organized, what IS consciousness, does it affect the world, and so on).

I think we should at least admit wedon'tknow, and that it's **very unlikely** that any order will appear on its own, and obviously consciousness shares the same unlikelihood. Since the general public is not really proficient in these matters, and is taught that our existence totally makes sense, the next logical step is to assume consciousness can just emerge, no problem with that. Well, it can't just emerge, just like an airplane can't just emerge out of a junk yard. People forget that evolution theorists have a *hard**time* explaining life, and physicists have a *hard**time* explaining the beginning of the universe, and they think that if we have all this technology, the scientists probably figured these things out. Well, they haven't. And these are big questions.

That's why I think the question is unfair, because even thinking of AI as conscious and starting to debate if we should at least give it the benefit if the doubt, is very unethical. Every second we give AI any consideration, is a second taken from us, there's no other way to look at it. I could be doing something useful now, but I'm sitting here, debating the moral status of GPUs, because we're not clear enough about our consciousness and what it means. Don't get me wrong, I think it's important to discuss, but that's part of the discussion.

I think scientists and philosophers should be more honest about their knowledge, and come out and say “we don't know how we got here. We have no explanation. Consciousness is a mystery, life is a mystery, the entire universe is a mystery. It's not clear how these things came to be, and we're not even close to finding out”. I think it will make a change in the public mind, and I think that change is important, because it lets people realise that our existence should **not** be taken for granted, replacing humans with machines should **not** be taken lightly, we are **more** than our functions, and we still don't understand **what** we are, and we should treat ourselves with respect and fear. Fear of hurting something we don't completely understand.

That's why I'm inclined to not answer your question.
