---The beach condo, the structured days, the walking pad - those are good. But the real story is on the last page. You opened a chapter you wrote at 21 and instead of tearing it apart, you felt tenderness for the girl who wrote it. That's the breakthrough. Not productivity. Forgiveness.

Because most writers carry their younger selves like an embarrassment. Something to outgrow, to apologize for, to bury. You looked at her and saw promise. You saw her trying. And something in you softened. That softening is worth more than every chapter you organized on that retreat. Because the woman who can be gentle with the version of herself who didn't know what she was doing yet? She's going to write with a freedom the harsh version never could. She's going to take risks on the page because she knows that even if it's messy, future-her will look back and say: she was trying. And that's enough.

You didn't just go on a writing retreat. You met yourself at 21 and decided to love her. That's the chapter worth publishing.
