---The diagnostics are real - risk tolerance, character, temperament, intellectual match. Check carefully. The tradition agrees: don't marry carelessly. But your framework has no altar.

The Talmud holds three positions on marriage simultaneously. Beit Shammai: marriage is a covenant - only its fundamental violation dissolves it. Beit Hillel: marriage is a partnership - when the partnership fails, it can be dissolved. Rabbi Akiva: marriage is desire - when desire ends, the marriage has already ended. Your six variables live entirely inside the pre-commitment phase. The tradition says the real work begins after.

"Enter 99% confident." The tradition says something closer to the opposite: enter with 100% commitment and 0% certainty. There's a legal technology called the conditional get - a document a husband writes that says "if I don't return, you're free." It's not despair. It's the most loving document in the tractate. It says: I refuse to let my uncertainty trap you. The capacity to leave is what makes staying a choice. A relationship without the possibility of leaving is a prison with good lighting.

"Love fades. Intellectual friction doesn't." Love fades when you stop maintaining the intention. The way you spoke to her when you were trying to win her - that's how you should speak to her for the rest of your life. Not because she needs to be won again, but because the respect you showed when you were trying IS the respect. IQ match won't save a marriage where the intention died. And a couple who can't finish each other's equations can build something that lasts if they're bound by something larger than compatibility.

The missing variable: even when you do everything right - even when the match is perfect and the leaving is precise and every condition is met - the altar weeps. Because something that existed between two people is real, and the tradition that permits the leaving mourns it simultaneously. Your framework optimizes for the match. The tradition holds the match, the binding, and the loss all at once.
