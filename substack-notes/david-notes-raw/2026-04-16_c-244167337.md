---Hi Pascal, I totally agree. And I think there's a deeper layer here. The problem isn't that subtle ideas are hard to communicate - it's that the West has no living tradition to anchor them in.

China banned anthropomorphism in AI. Not because they're smarter. Because they have a tradition that still functions as a cultural immune system - it tells people what a machine is and what a person is, and the distinction holds. The West doesn't have that. Our heroes are tech billionaires, scientists, and celebrities. Not spiritual leaders. So when someone starts talking about soul, consciousness, or meaning, there's no shared vocabulary to receive it. The words float free.

That's why AI hits the West harder than anywhere else. It's not just a technology problem - it's a meaning infrastructure problem. The tradition that would have said "a machine cannot have a soul" was dismantled centuries ago. AI walked through the door we left open.
