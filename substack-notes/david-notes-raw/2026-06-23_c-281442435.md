---Good piece, and I agree the animal welfare conclusions stand on their own regardless of how the metaphysics shakes out. But I want to press on the part the essay treats as already settled, because I think it is the whole game.

Your evolutionary story runs on the claim that consciousness does causal work. The organisms with "better perceptions, predictions, reflexes, planned actions, patience, and coordination" thrive, and consciousness is what gets selected because it confers those advantages. That only works if the felt, first-person character of experience changes what the organism does. And here is the problem: if the physical story is causally complete, which is what a naturalist is committed to, then the neural mechanism already accounts for every movement, every avoidance, every plan. The phenomenal feel is not adding any behavior on top of what the wiring already produces. A physically identical creature with the lights off, doing all the same information processing but with nothing it is like to be it, would perceive, predict, flee, and reproduce exactly as well. Selection cannot see the difference between that creature and you, because by hypothesis there is no behavioral difference for it to act on.

So the evolutionary account beautifully explains the mechanisms. What it does not explain, and cannot explain in this form, is why any of it is accompanied by experience at all. Natural selection optimizes function. If experience is causally inert with respect to function, selection is blind to it.

The one move that escapes this is the one you gesture at with emergence: say consciousness just is the relevant processing, so there is no possible duplicate with the lights off. Fine, but then you owe an account of why that processing is constituted as experience, and "emergence" does not provide it. It names the gap rather than closing it. Either consciousness is something over and above the function, in which case it is causally idle and unselectable, or it is identical to the function, in which case the hard problem is sitting right where you left it, now wearing the word "emergent." The essay needs one of those horns to be false and argues for neither.

Not a defense of dualism, just a claim that the selection-for-consciousness story is doing less work than it looks like it is doing.
