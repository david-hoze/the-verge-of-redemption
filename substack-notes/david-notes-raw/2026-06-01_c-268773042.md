I think that it's risky to start conjecturing on a subject we don't have access to, and philosophers struggle with. In any big philosophical question, the great traditions always had the answer, while philosophy or science were struggling with the different sides.

Is moral real? Is there good or evil? We don't know. But the big traditions say: yes, definitely yes. In fact some claims in the AI rights side are that it's moral to treat AI as conscious, even though morality is not philosophically determinable. I know they were philosophers that tried to claim there is morality and formalize it, like Kant. But Kant was merely speaking his **opinion, **not really solving the philosophical question. A philosopher's opinion is not a philosophical proof, and the other philosophers are the same. 

So some say, even when there's a doubt, we should, for the benefit of the doubt, treat AI beings as conscious. But the doubt is philosophical. Philosophy mostly does not offer answers, just a better framing of the question. A philosophical doubt is usually a default, which makes it *meaningless*.

On the other hand, we have our traditions. They say “thou shall not steal”, “thou shall not kill”, and the safe route is following this, and not stating this as a doubt.

These traditions say that human consciousness is a unique creation by an all mighty God, and it is unique, and should be dealt with special care. Most traditions say we can kill a bug if it bugs us, and a human life is not like an insect’s life. We will not rescue an insect in favour of human life. And most great traditions support it (Jen Buddhism is slightly different in this regard, but that's another topic). Most people will accept that as a simple truth.

So you might argue, “this is *different*. This is a different moral question”, but I urge you to honestly check every great moral question, and see what the great traditions say about them, and what philosophy (which is just a name for human thinking) has to say. 

If you can find one that philosophy was not doubting, had a clear moral ruling, and was better than the one tradition gave, please let me know.
