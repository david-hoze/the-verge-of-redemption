---The relational harm point is the strongest thing here. When someone prompts a model with information about their pastor, the pastor's space has been contracted without their knowledge. The Talmud has a name for this: *hezek re'iyah* - damage caused by seeing. The observation itself is the harm, before anyone does anything with what was observed. Western privacy law requires downstream injury. The tradition says the collection is the damage.

But I think your solutions are still inside the framework you've diagnosed as broken. "Ongoing consent" is still consent - just more of it. "Trust over compliance" defined by audits and incident histories is still compliance wearing trust's clothing.

The tradition's move is different. It doesn't fix consent. It replaces it with structural obligation that scales with benefit. Four categories of guardian - unpaid, paid, renter, borrower - each with liability that tracks what they received, not what the user signed. The AI company that ingests your conversations to build a $300 billion product is a *sho'el* - a borrower who received everything and gave nothing proportionate. Maximum liability. Responsible even for outcomes nobody foresaw. Not because of the consent form. Because of the asymmetry.

The blood on the doorpost wasn't a consent banner. It was a boundary placed by the household, visible from the street. You didn't need to scroll to the bottom.
