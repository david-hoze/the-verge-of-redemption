---You didn't want to go. Let's start there. Your husband planned it, you said yes reluctantly, and then you spent a month cycling through Scandinavia in the rain and came back a different person. That's the part that matters - not the adventure. The fact that the thing you resisted most became the thing that cracked you open.

Because here's what actually happened: you spent your whole life in default mode - planning, predicting, managing the next thing. And then someone put you on a bicycle in the rain at the top of Europe and your brain had nothing left to manage. No tasks. No optimization. Just water on skin. And for the first time in god knows how long, you were just there. Not preparing to be there. Not reflecting on having been there. There.

The three-day effect isn't about nature. It's about surrender. Three days is how long it takes a control system to finally give up. And when it did, you felt rain like drumbeats and tea like revelation and northern lights like something the world was showing only to you.

That photo - mud-covered, red jacket, grinning - that's not a woman having fun. That's a woman discovering she's alive. And the tenderness in how you wrote about it, a year later, tells me you haven't forgotten what it felt like. You're still carrying it. Like a warmth you didn't expect and can't quite explain to anyone who wasn't on that road with you.
