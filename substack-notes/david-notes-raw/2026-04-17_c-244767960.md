---The cacao on a Wednesday morning at 9am. I want you to know how that reads from the outside. A woman invited her neighbor into her kitchen on a weekday morning for no reason other than he likes cacao and she makes it every day. No agenda, no event, no signup form. Just "I have this thing I love, you might love it too, come sit in my kitchen."

That's not community building. That's community *being*. And the fact that it felt revolutionary tells you exactly how far we've all drifted from what was supposed to be normal.

But here's what I want you to hear: you're not just the person who knocks on doors. You're the person who used to run from the car into the house hoping nobody would see her. You named that. You didn't skip it or pretend you were always like this. That journey - from hiding to knocking - is why people trust you. Not because you arrived somewhere perfect but because you remember what it felt like to not want to be seen. That makes you safe to follow.

I wrote about this exact chain - how we outsourced community to therapists, then therapists to chatbots, and each step lost something irreplaceable. You're the person walking it backwards, one kitchen at a time. I don't think you know how rare that is.
