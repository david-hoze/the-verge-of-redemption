---I almost scrolled past this because I've read fifty versions of "women wake up in midlife wanting more." Then I hit your distinction between crisis and reckoning and stopped. Because most people use those interchangeably, and you just proved they're opposites. A crisis is what happens when the wall falls on you. A reckoning is what happens when you realize you built the wall yourself.

But here's where I think you pulled the punch: you framed overfunctioning as a survival mechanism that becomes unsustainable. It's more than that. It's a bargain. Women trade their desires for indispensability, and the deal works - until they realize nobody asked them to sign it. The reckoning isn't discovering you want more. It's discovering you were the one who decided you weren't allowed to.

"Wanting more is a sign that you're alive" - that's true, but it's also the easy line. The harder one is: wanting more is terrifying because it means admitting that everything you built while not wanting was a choice you made. And that's the part most of your clients probably cry about.
