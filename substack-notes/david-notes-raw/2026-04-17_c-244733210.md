---"Real isn't how you are made. It's a thing that happens to you." You picked the right quote. The honesty about shedding identities that were self-destructive - through food, through performance, through making yourself smaller for other people - that's not a metaphor about caterpillars. That's a woman who looked at what she'd built around herself and decided it wasn't her. That takes more courage than most people will ever need.

The caterpillar is the right image, but it's even more radical than you're letting it be. The caterpillar doesn't gradually become a butterfly. It dissolves. Completely. Inside the chrysalis there's nothing recognizable. Then something new is built from what remained. You already did that. The piece you wrote is proof. The woman writing this isn't the one who was disappearing into other people's expectations. That woman is gone.

There's a teaching that changed how I think about this: you are not the person who failed yesterday. That person was created yesterday. The person who exists now was created now. Not unfolding - remade. Every morning. Which means the wreckage behind you belongs to someone who no longer exists. And the woman writing today was always on her way. I wrote about what that daily re-creation looks like here: [https://davidhoze.substack.com/p/the-person-who-is-not-here-anymore](https://davidhoze.substack.com/p/the-person-who-is-not-here-anymore)
