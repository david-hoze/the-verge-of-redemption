---The line that stopped me: "someone who helps people understand themselves through what they wear." Most people would dismiss that as surface. It's not. The exterior shapes the interior - and the oldest traditions knew this.

In the tradition I come from, the priests couldn't serve without their specific garments. Not as costume - without the clothing, the person literally couldn't become who they were supposed to be. The garments made the kohein. There's a special set of clothes for the Sabbath - you change what you're wearing and the day changes. The body registers the transition through what covers it. And there are fringes on every garment as reminders - "you shall see them and remember." The seeing comes through the body first.

What Leigh is doing - helping someone stand in front of a mirror and recognize themselves - isn't styling. It's the moment the exterior finally matches what was always inside. That's sacred work whether she calls it that or not.
