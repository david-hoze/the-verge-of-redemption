## 1. Religion isn't on science's playing field

**Thesis:** Religion is not a competing explanation to science - it *asserts*, it doesn't *explain*, so "beating a religious explanation" is a category error. David's faith rests on a covenant, not a hypothesis: the Israelites left Egypt and accepted the Torah after undeniable miracles, and trust was requested only *after* that was established - like a diploma you aren't retested for. Falsifiability comes through prophecy ("this war was prophesied"). So science isn't measured against a weak religious opponent to defeat; it stands alone, measured only against our own reasoning for validity.

**Tested phrasings:** "Beating a religious explanation is impossible, because religion doesn't explain, it just asserts. No science, just a contract: I saved you, you are now my people." / "After I graduated I'm not supposed to go through all the tests again." / Atheist still owes an answer to "why is there anything at all?" - and the explanation-regress forces a first cause that needs no explanation.

**Graph meme:** `religion-asserts-not-explains` (meme-94). Relates to `philosophy-is-a-commentary-that-forgot`.

## 2. Free will as the hack that was never performed

**Thesis:** Free will isn't *proven*, it's *strong empirical testimony* - on the order of P != NP. If humans were deterministic there would be a way to hack a person into acting against their will (not via fraud or persuasion of inclinations, but literally changing what they want), and to do it at scale and predictably, the way we hack LLMs. In all of human history it has never been done; our entire civilization is built on respecting human freedom because you cannot get peace by overriding it. The absence of the hack is the evidence.

**Tested phrasings:** "It's not proof, it's an empirical testimony. It's going to be worth a lot of money if someone manages to do it." / Marketing/neuromarketing works on inclinations, not on overriding the will. / Pairs with the performative contradiction (denying free will requires using it).

**Movie note:** needs an opener not yet used. **Graph meme:** `free-will-empirical-testimony` (meme-95).

## 3. Identity is consciousness, not the body or continuity

**Thesis:** A person's identity is his consciousness - not his body, not psychological/numerical continuity. An unconscious body (anesthesia, sleep) is not a person; personhood is *off*, like a cell with its nucleus removed. We only borrow the anesthetized person's identity because we know consciousness will return; the skeleton in Einstein's grave is not Einstein. Identity-first frameworks (the "Identology" debate) conflate a cell's practical persistence with a person's, which is consciousness. A man can lose an arm, his memories, or his intelligence and remain himself.

**Tested phrasings:** "When I'm unconscious, I'm not myself. An unconscious body is not a person." / "Cells don't have a 'self'." / The body is consciousness's percept/garment, not its bearer.

**Interlocutor:** Identology (@undercognitiveload), "Before Consciousness" series - see `substack-people/respondents/identology/`. **Graph meme:** `identity-is-consciousness` (meme-96).
