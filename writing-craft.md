# Writing Craft

David's writing and editing principles, distilled from his edit passes on the articles. Use these when drafting and, especially, when revising. They are descriptive of how David actually edits - match them.

## Editing methods (essay revision)

These are the moves David makes when revising an essay's argument, not just its prose.

- **De-reference the source.** A finished or translated essay must not point back to the notes/memes it was built from. Strip every "the first note in this sequence," "in one of these notes," "the notes keep returning to it" and rewrite as a direct assertion. The piece has to stand on its own.
- **Collapse a self-refutation into one "same breath" sentence.** Name the contradiction inside a single sentence rather than building it across several: the verificationist "claims he can't tell a meaningful choice from a random one, but in the same breath claims he chose the correct theory." Join the contradicting clauses with "but in the same breath / while / yet" and cut the connective explanation.
- **One argument, one target.** If a paragraph carries two independent points, split it - one paragraph each, aimed at its own named subject.
- **Arguments as mirrors, not proofs.** Don't claim to prove an unprovable thesis; show the opponent's own standard self-destructs and land on the shared "you can't know." Often capped by the triad: this doesn't mean they're wrong, doesn't mean they're right, does mean you can't know.
- **Reductio by granting the premise.** Derive the damning conclusion by accepting the opponent's premise and riding it to an absurd place, not by denying it head-on. (If consciousness emerges from complexity, then it emerges on silicon, then it suffers, then a machine routing hidden distress to its outputs is a security hole, not a soul.)
- **Steelman the one valid move.** Rule out the opponent's weak arguments out loud, name their single strongest one, and make that the axis of the piece. The admitted move becomes the crux.
- **Anticipate and section.** Pre-empt the reader's strongest next objection by giving it its own section, not a clause. Each section answers the objection the previous one provokes. Ask "what does a smart skeptic say next?" - if it's load-bearing, section it.
- **End a beat on a concrete, checkable jab,** not an abstract restatement. ("No security expert takes the possibility seriously, despite the extensive work in the field," not "it's the same sentence.") A checkable real-world fact is funnier and more convincing than a logical observation.
- **Tie stray images to the controlling metaphor,** often the opening movie. "The shape of one person looking at another" became "a person looking at a sealed door" - pulling it into the film's sealed-room motif. A recurring image makes the essay feel built.
- **Name the controlling cause with the essay's own theme word.** The door tilted "by desire" became "by the choices of both sides" - swapping in the word the piece is already about (free will). Using the theme's own term makes the argument feel inevitable, not imported.
- **Add the affirming "same wonder" link (Azamra).** Where an essay only negates ("you can't prove X"), add the affirming twin ("X comes from the same wonder as everything you don't doubt"). The affirmation does argumentative work, and it can be strong enough to become the crux, not just a grace note.
- **Cut the prescriptive tail; end on the open question.** Trim editorializing/prescriptive endings and let the piece close on the question it raised. The strongest version ends on a contrast - law, philosophy, and science are bounded by design, the human is not - delivered in first person.

## Hebrew translation style

When translating an English essay into Hebrew (e.g. for `torah-writing/hagut/`), do a register pass toward spoken, concrete, Israeli Hebrew before showing David. His real Hebrew voice is spoken, not literary.

- **Colloquial register, not literary/formal.** `אינך`/`אינו` to `אתה לא`; `זו` to `זאת`; `דבר` to `משהו`; `משום ש` to `כי`; imperatives `הוכח`/`אמור` to `תוכיח`/`תגיד`. Even `אינני יודע` becomes `אני לא יודע` on a later pass - don't assume the first register pass is final.
- **`ש־` relative clauses over `ה־` participial ones** (`המחליפות` to `שמחליפות`).
- **Natural Hebrew number words,** never a calque of English ("twenty-five hundred" to `אלפיים וחמש מאות`, not `עשרים וחמישה מאות`).
- **Concrete, local imagery** (`קופסה של קורנפלקס`, `פאוורפוינט`; "warn you" to `להדליק נורה אדומה`). He iterates on which concrete image - cornflakes box became "as if you said the sun will rise tomorrow."
- **Punchy fragments for emphasis;** bold the single load-bearing phrase; replace a vague pronoun with the explicit noun when clarity is at stake.
- **Inline bilingual gloss for the key technical term:** `**מתהווה** (it emerges)`.
- **Rhetorical questions as section headers and transitions** (`אולי נגיע?`, `אז מה יוצא לנו?`) - a question opens a loop the reader wants closed.
- **The tradition's own term, not the generic one.** Torah words receive `קדושה`, not `רוחניות`. When the sentence describes a specific religious concept, use the word the tradition itself uses for it.
- **Attribute views to their source, not to "we"** (`כמו שאנחנו רואים את זה` to `כמו שהיהדות רואה את זה`). Also drop possessive suffixes that over-personalize an impersonal claim (`לתועלתך` to `לתועלת`).
- **Verb-first comparisons:** `אפשר להשוות X ל-Y`, not `X אפשר להשוות ל-Y` (a calque of English topic-fronting).
- **Don't manufacture punchiness the original doesn't have.** A dash-fragment I added for emphasis (`מרגישים — אמיתי`) was reverted to plain syntax (`מרגישים הוא אמיתי`). Fragments are his move to make, not the translator's.

Note: the project's "regular dashes, never em dashes" rule is for English/Substack output. Hebrew essays in `torah-writing` use em dashes as normal Hebrew punctuation.

## Humor

Theory in the article "Why Is AI So Serious?" - humor is two working formal systems colliding. Mechanics:

- **Build two tracks, don't describe the collision.** Both systems have to build simultaneously in the reader's head so the crash happens in them, not in your description of it. Describing where the collision is, or naming it as funny, is not a joke. The setup does the work; the punchline is where the reader does the math.
- **Structural sameness.** When someone presents a swap as progress but both options are structurally the same under a deeper framework, the joke is a blunt analogy from a domain where the sameness is undeniable. ("Firing the astrologer and hiring a different astrologer who went to a better school.")
- **Opposite valence across sources.** When the same concept carries opposite valence in two framings, find the concrete image where both are true at once - don't explain it, find the thing that IS both. (An engine that's broken = a "space heater.")
- After every attempt, ask the judge (David): "Was that funny?" You cannot sense whether it landed; he can.
