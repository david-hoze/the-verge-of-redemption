# Identology

**Profile:** https://substack.com/@undercognitiveload

> Stub from the May-June 2026 notes likes/replies review. Flesh out on next engagement.

## Who

Writes the "Before Consciousness" series (under @undercognitiveload). Civil, systematic interlocutor on personal identity.

## Position

**Identity-first.** A thing can be a thing without being conscious; consciousness is downstream of identity, not its ground. Uses anesthesia as the intuition pump: you count down from ten, wake in recovery, and our practices treat that as the same individual - so numerical identity persists through unconsciousness. "Importance and ontological priority don't necessarily coincide": consciousness may be personhood's most valuable feature without being what individuates the bearer.

## Where he clashes with David

David's counter (a live debate worth turning into an article): **identity is consciousness, not the body or psychological continuity.** An unconscious body - anesthesia, sleep - is not a person; personhood is off, like a cell with its nucleus removed. We only borrow the anesthetized person's identity because we know consciousness returns; the skeleton in Einstein's grave is not Einstein. Identology says "it-self" of a cell - but cells have no self. A man can lose an arm, his memories, or his intelligence and remain himself; what persists is consciousness. Treating a cell's practical persistence and a person's as the same conflation causes errors downstream.
