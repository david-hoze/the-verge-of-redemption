"Durable hostility to functional sovereignty" is one of the sharpest phrases I've read on Substack this year. You've named something that gets described all the time but rarely with this precision - the specific mechanism by which postcolonial discourse punishes states that actually build things.

The AFCON section lands because you're tracking something that happened organically. Nobody coordinated the "Long live Israel" moment. Nobody ran a campaign. Ordinary Moroccans watched conditional solidarity collapse in real time and said what they actually thought. That's the most honest kind of data.

Here's the question your piece opened in me that I can't close: what's underneath Morocco's realism? You show convincingly that the pan-movements substituted romance for statecraft. Morocco refused the romance and chose functional sovereignty instead. But sovereignty toward what? If the answer is just "order" or "development" or "not being captured by ideology" - that's a negation, not a destination.

I tried to work through this same problem from a different angle - why every great resistance movement organized around meaning that was already there before the coordination started, and what happens when the fire comes and there's no architecture underneath. The French Resistance had France. The civil rights movement had the image of God. Morocco has something too. Your piece gets close to it but doesn't name it.
