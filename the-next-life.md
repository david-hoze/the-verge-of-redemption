# The Next Life

*An interpretation of the movie “Past Lives”*

*Past Lives (2023). Directed by Celine Song. Starring Greta Lee, Teo Yoo, John Magaro. Two children in Seoul who might have been each other's grow up on opposite sides of the world, and when the boy finally crosses the ocean to find the woman she became, she is already married and he is twelve years too late. Nobody betrays anyone; the timing simply fails. What is left is a Korean word for the people you were bound to across lifetimes, and a question about whatever life comes next.*

“Past Lives” leaves us with an unresolved emotion. Nora is clearly in love with Hae Sung. She’s always smiling with excitement when she sees him, and treats Arthur like an old friend, and intimate companion. She loves Arthur, and she says it. But when they talk in bed about her leaving him for Hae Sung, she doesn’t say “you’re the love of my life, he’s just a childhood sweetheart”. She says “don’t you know me, I would never miss my rehearsals for some dude”. And he says, painfully, “Yes, I know you”.

So why don’t they end up together? She bursts into tears when he leaves, and goes to Arthur. Because it’s too late. She is not going to throw away her life for Hae Sung, when she loves Arthur. Hae Sung was replaced, because he arrived 12 years too late. And he was replaced by a man he likes. And that hurts the most. Because he knows she can’t leave him. He knows she won’t take a Korean guy, set with his Korean views, barely knowing a word in English. He came too late. At least for this life.

Hae Sung had his chance. 12 years earlier they talked through Skype for a long time, almost every day. This wasn’t some long distance fantasy. It was a daily conversation that they arranged their day around, and never got tired. They had real connection, and they were excited every day around that. But when she asks him to move to New York, he says “Why should I move to New York?”. He has his plans, his steady Korean career, his trip to China. She falls for him, up to the point when she can no longer concentrate on her goals, and he doesn’t understand. She asks him to stop the talking. Not because she doesn’t love him, because she loves him too much and can’t stop. “Why am I being like this?”, he says with tears in his eyes, “we weren’t dating or anything”. And he accepts it. He doesn’t fight for her, he doesn’t sacrifice. He doesn’t understand what he’s feeling.

Even when he finally comes, too late, to New York, when she asks him why he came, he still answers “I had thoughts”. But at the bar scene, he finally understands. Nora is someone who leaves. Korea is too small for her ambitions. She can’t stay there, and never will. He loves her still, even though that little girl is gone, as Nora explains to him. But he sees Arthur, a truly good man, that knows who Nora is, and is willing to support her whatever happens. And he likes him. And it hurts him the most, because he knows he truly missed her.

So, what’s going on? Is this a tragedy? Not quite, the movie has a concept, “in-yun”. People meet together because they had past lives. In this life, Hae Sung and Nora don’t get to be together. But his last words for her are, “What do you think we’ll be in the next one?”. They don’t know. But they learned their lesson in this life. Nora learned that Hae Sung _can_ love her as she is now, and he would change for her, if he understood what she meant to him early enough. And Hae Sung learns that these thoughts, these feelings, are not something that you can replace with a steady job and a girlfriend in Korea. It’s real connection. Nora loves Arthur, he is the man for her in this life. Hae Sung is late. But they have a consolation. They have a future life, where they learned their lesson, and can finally be together.
